## 1. Program at a glance

- **Purpose.** The program funds the early build of a product to show its intended functions and design. The phase is for experimenting, testing and validating ideas until there is a first functional prototype. The product still needs a production phase before it can be sold. (PG p.4 s.2.1, p.5 s.3.2.1)
- **Selection.** Selective and competitive, scored on an evaluation grid. (PG p.4, p.7 s.5)
- **Maximum contribution.** The lesser of 75% of Eligible Costs or $250,000. (PG p.6 s.4.1)
- **Higher maximum for past success.** The lesser of 75% or $350,000, if a previously released IDM project earned $500,000 gross or sold or downloaded 20,000 units. Prototypes, demos, test content and game jams don't count. (PG p.6 s.4.1.1) Phantom Break has no released title, so the cap is $250,000.
- **To request the full $250,000**, Eligible Costs must be at least $333,334. The applicant then covers at least $83,334 (25%).
- **Form of funding.** It is not a grant. It is a non-interest-bearing advance that is repayable. If the CMF later funds production through Innovation and Experimentation or Commercial Projects, the advance becomes a recoupable investment in production. If the CMF does not fund production, the applicant either repays the whole advance by the first day of production, or asks the CMF to convert it into a recoupable investment. (CORE p.9 s.4.1.1, 4.1.1.1)
- **Sale of the IP.** If the project or its IP is sold or transferred to an unrelated party, the whole CMF contribution must be repaid at once. (CORE p.10 s.4.3)
- **Lifetime cap per project.** $1.5 million across all CMF programs. (CORE p.10 s.4.2.2)
- **Payment schedule.** 80% when the CMF agreement is signed, 20% when closing documents are approved. The CMF can change the split case by case. (RD p.3)
- **Deadlines.** Two submission windows, evaluation "Selective" (not first-come, first-served): April 28 to May 14, 2026, and October 27 to November 10, 2026 (cmf-fmc.ca/program/prototyping-program/, "Dates to remember", checked 2026-10-22). Every application submitted inside a window is scored together after it closes, so submit any time before November 10. PG p.5 s.3.2.2(c) says the CMF may commit up to 50% of the program budget at the First Closing Date, and the rest at the Second. The closing time of day isn't on the page. **Confirm it with cmf.fmc.coordination@telefilm.ca.**
- **Pre-application consultation.** Required only for new applicants, meaning a company that has never received CMF IDM funding. Requests go in at least two weeks before the deadline. (CORE p.6 s.3.1.1) Phantom Break has received IDM funding twice (section 7), so it is not a new applicant. The Dialogue guide still recommends a consultation for all applicants. (RD p.8)
- **Decision timeline.** Not stated in the sources. Past data: the 2023-24 prototyping application went in on 2023-10-10 and the refusal letter is dated 2024-01-22, about 3.5 months later (`R24\decision_327868_20240122.pdf` p.1, `R24\FEEDBACK - 327868 - Project Dawn (Working Title).pdf` p.1).
- **Duration.** No maximum is stated. The prototyping start date in Dialogue can't be more than 3 months before the deadline you apply to. Start and end dates must match the budget period. (RD p.11 Appendix 2) The 2024 application used 12 months (2024-08-01 to 2025-07-31).
- **Submission.** Online through Dialogue, Telefilm's portal. A started application is deleted if not submitted within 60 days. You must tick the Statement box to submit. The CMF sends no acknowledgement letter. Questions go to cmf.fmc.coordination@telefilm.ca. (RD p.7-8)
- **Limits.** One project per program per deadline period (CORE p.7 s.3.2, PG p.5 s.3.1). At most two open Prototyping contracts. Earlier projects' final costs documents, including a cost report, must be delivered and the files closed before you apply with a new project (CORE p.6 s.3.1.1, PG p.5 s.3.1). A project gets only one round of Prototyping funding in its life (PG p.5 s.3.2.2(a)).
- **Two refusals bar a project.** A project refused twice or more by the same program activity since 2010-11 can't apply to that activity again. (PG p.5 s.3.2.2(b)) Project Dawn was refused Prototyping twice, so it can never apply to Prototyping again. ProjectRunner is a different project and is not affected.
- **Regional cap.** No one zone gets more than 60% of IDM spending in a year. Alberta is in "The West" zone. (CORE p.11 s.5)

## 2. Eligibility

### Applicant (CORE p.6 s.3.1)

- A for-profit, taxable Canadian corporation.
- Canadian-controlled under sections 26 to 28 of the Investment Canada Act.
- Head office in Canada.
- At least one shareholder with at least 1 year of full-time professional experience making IDM, film or TV content. Work done while in post-secondary school doesn't count.
- In good standing with the relevant guilds and associations.
- Has signed the Narrative Positioning attestation.
- Owns and controls all rights needed to produce and exploit the project. Service companies that don't own the rights can't apply. (CORE p.6 s.3.1.1)
- "Applicant" includes related parties and parent companies. (CORE p.6)
- The sources set no minimum years in business and no track-record minimum. Track record is scored under Team instead (PG p.7).
- An applicant in default with the CMF can be refused. (APPB p.5 Chapter 1 s.5)

### Project (CORE p.7-8 s.3.2, PG p.5 s.3.2)

- New interactive content tied to the Canadian cultural sector, made to entertain or inform, and meant to be widely sold or distributed through standard digital platforms. (CORE p.7)
- Videogames are an eligible content type. (CORE p.7 s.3.2.2.1)
- **Canadian elements.** Canadians own the underlying rights and develop them in a significant way. The project is conceived and produced in Canada. At least 75% of Eligible Costs are Canadian costs. Ownership and executive, creative and financial control are Canadian. (CORE p.7 s.3.2.1) There is no Canadian content points scale for IDM; the 75% Canadian cost rule is the test. The budget Detail tab repeats it: "75% of expenses must be of Canadian origin." (BUD Detail)
- **Interactivity.** The whole experience must involve a significant degree of interaction. Linear parts are allowed. (CORE p.8 s.3.2.2.3)
- **AI.** The applicant must make sure Canadians own and meaningfully develop all underlying rights, and that there is clear access to them. The CMF's AI Guiding Principles are encouraged. (CORE p.8 s.3.2.3(d)) The OCA description must describe any generative AI use and name the tools. (RD p.3)
- No excessive violence, hate propaganda, sexual violence or exploitation, or obscene content under the Criminal Code. (CORE p.8 s.3.2.3(a)) Dialogue asks you to agree to this. (RD p.9)
- International coproductions follow COPRO. Each coproducer pays at least 20% (two countries) or 10% (three or more). (COPRO p.2 s.4)

### Prototype vs conceptualization vs production

- **Prototyping.** For a project that already has "a solid concept and design" and needs testing before production. It must end in "a functioning prototype build". (PG p.5 s.3.2.1)
- **Conceptualization.** From 2026-27 it is open to new applicants only. (SOC p.5) Phantom Break is not a new applicant, so Conceptualization is closed to it for any project, including Project Echo (section 7).
- **Production.** Commercial Projects (Main Stream and the new Third-Party Investment Stream), Innovation and Experimentation, and Iteration. (CORE p.5, SOC p.5) Dialogue asks which production program the project targets. (RD p.9)

### Ineligible projects (CORE p.7-8 s.3.2.2.2)

Corporate, industrial or promotional projects. Curriculum-based or e-learning. Internal-use only. Commissioned or service-contract work. Partial or split projects that can't be exploited alone. Ports or platform adaptations. Compilations of repurposed material. System software. Pure R&D that would qualify for SR&ED or IRAP. Real-money gambling. Gamification of non-cultural content. Digital marketplaces. Social media platforms. Linear or audio-only projects such as podcasts.

## 4. Evaluation criteria and weights

Two stages (PG p.7 s.5):

1. Every eligible project is scored on Originality, Creativity and Advancement (OCA) first. Only projects scoring 45 or more out of 60 go on.
2. Stage two scores the remaining criteria.

The CMF may refuse to fund projects below a minimum score in a round, and it won't accept material changes after you apply. (CORE p.11 s.5)

## 5. Budget rules

### Eligible costs (PG p.6 s.4.2)

- Research and preparation of content.
- Salaries, wages and contracts for the project team: project management, business development, coding, design, infrastructure and content.
- Technology hardware and software. Equipment and software must be prorated to project use and amortized. (BUD Detail line 11)
- Costs to put content online, including copyright clearance, documentation, prototype design and development, and translation.
- Travel and accommodation.
- Project audit fees.
- Other technical and administrative costs.
- Marketing and promotion.
- Reasonable costs for environmentally sustainable practices.
- Immersive testing costs, as above.

### Ineligible costs and limits

- Core operations and capital spending such as rent, real estate and maintenance. (CORE p.10 s.4.4)
- The CMF removes costs it finds excessive, inflated or unreasonable. (CORE p.10 s.4.4)
- Rights can't be paid to the applicant, a co-applicant, a parent company or a related person. (BUD Detail line 02)
- Application preparation costs (line 03) must be real, verifiable and incurred before you submit. (BUD Detail line 03)
- Costs must be before taxes, in whole numbers. (BUD Detail)
- Only costs incurred or paid by the applicant count. At final costs only actual, verifiable costs count. (BUD Instructions)

### Marketing floor and ceiling

Marketing, audience engagement and community-building must be at least 10% and at most 25% of budget sections B + C, measured before those costs. (PG p.6) The budget adds three rules (BUD Detail lines 10.50 to 10.95, BUD Instructions):

- The spending must fall inside the prototyping period.
- It can't be reduced at final costs.
- At least 50% must be of Canadian origin.

### Producer fee, overhead, contingency

- The producer line (01.05) can't exceed 10% of B + C if the producer is a shareholder. (BUD Detail line 01)
- A shareholder who acts as project manager must be budgeted at 01.05, not 04.05. (BUD Detail line 04)
- Corporate overhead (F) can't exceed 10% of B + C. (BUD Detail)
- Contingency (G) can't exceed 10% of B + C. (BUD Detail)
- For IDM, producer fees and corporate overhead must be reasonable and "no such expense may exceed 10% of Sections B+C". (APPB p.34 PFCO s.D)
- Administration costs (section E) must be project-specific. Everyday company costs go in overhead (F). (BUD Detail line 15)

### Internal and related-party costs

- Every cost is tagged Internal (payroll or paid to the applicant), Related (a parent company, or a person who controls or has significant influence), or External (freelancers and unrelated companies). (BUD Instructions)
- Internal and related costs are shown at actual cost, with no profit margin and no taxes. (BUD Detail note, BUD Summary signature text)
- Internal and related salaries must be hourly, daily, weekly or monthly, not flat. (BUD Detail, BUD Instructions)
- Disclose all related-party transactions at budget and at final costs. (CORE p.10 s.4.4.1, APPB p.11 s.3, p.13 s.4.1.2)
- Related-party costs must be reasonable, directly tied to this phase, and paid within 120 days of completion unless you are waiting on a tax credit. Measure them at actual flow-through cost or fair value. (APPB p.13 s.4.1.3)
- Related-party labour needs T4s, contracts and signed timesheets that name the project. (APPB p.14 s.4.1.5)
- Spouses and immediate family count as related parties. (APPB p.11-12 s.4.1.1 and examples) Satomi Brandt's pay would be a related cost.
- If the applicant runs other business, internal allocations are treated like related-party transactions. (APPB p.14 s.4.2)

### Interim financing

- A third-party lender's rate is expected to match market rates. Above-market rates must be justified, and the CMF can reject them. (APPB p.19 s.4.12.1)
- A related party can charge Bank of Canada prime + 1%. (APPB p.20 s.4.12.2)

### Financing structure

- The CMF pays up to 75%. The rest comes from the applicant. (PG p.6)
- Cash flow inflow lines: CMF Advance, Applicant Investment, Deferrals (shareholders only), Distributor, Federal Tax Credit, Provincial Tax Credit, and three Other Funding lines. (BUD Cash Flow rows 43-51)
- Deferred salaries are accepted only for employed shareholders. (RD p.3, BUD Instructions)
- Loans and lines of credit are not eligible financing sources. (RD p.11)
- The cash flow covers only the prototyping period. You can add month columns past one year, up to the last CMF payment. (RD p.3, BUD Instructions)

## 6. Deliverables at the end of the prototype

- **A functioning prototype build.** It is not a finished product. (PG p.5 s.3.2.1)
- **The materials you list in Dialogue** under "Materials to be delivered at the Final Costs stage". Dialogue's examples are a vertical slice, a number of levels and characters, systems to be built, a number of minutes, and market research. (RD p.9) The 2024 list was a game design document, a GitHub repo, the final cost report, a playable PC prototype, the movement system, character designs, enemies, a boss fight, a village, arenas and puzzle mechanics (`2024-ProjectSilence\2024-06-18\Complete Application _ Compléter la demande - Project Dawn.pdf` p.2).
- **Written reports** on the Community Engagement Plan and Sustainability Plan, confirming the work done. (PG p.7, CEP p.1, SP p.1)
- **Cost reporting depends on budget size.** (APPB p.10 Chapter 2 s.2.1)
  - Under $250,000: an uncertified final cost report plus a producer declaration (CMF form).
  - $250,000 to $499,999: a final certified activity cost statement with an independent accountant's Review Engagement Report.
  - $500,000 or more: an audited statement.
- **Final cost report rules.** Use the standard format and explain big variances from the budget. Disclose related-party transactions, including ones not expected at budget. Keep separate project accounts. (APPB p.9 s.2, p.13 s.4.1.2)
- **Records.** Keep project records for 5 years. (APPB p.10 s.2.2)
- **The 20% holdback** is paid when the closing documents are approved. (RD p.3)
- **Past example.** The Project Silence conceptualization closing package had a signed Final Report Applicant Declaration (final costs $20,300, CMF file 330815, agreement dated 2024-07-31), a Project Summary, and a Project Intentions note (`2024-ProjectSilence\2025-02-15\docs\`).
