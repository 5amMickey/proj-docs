## Draft text

### Summary

Project Runner is a side-on cyberpunk narrative game in five episodes for Windows PC, made in Unreal Engine 5.8 by Phantom Break Inc. in Calgary. Chase Roberts, an unlicensed cyberware medic, opens a back-room clinic in NoWhere, a lawless city with no other medical care, while hiding that he wrote the kill code in every implant he repairs (draft). The player walks through side-on streets and rooms, looks at and uses objects, hacks salvaged implants, listens in on gangs, and makes timed dialogue choices on three buttons, where silence is the default.

The game has no morality meter. A ledger on the clinic desk records who Chase treated, who paid and who he turned away, and that record decides who helps him when the kill code fires (draft). What the player looked at adds options to later conversations. Each character counts both trust and how often Chase stayed silent. Patients Chase treated wear a marker in the street crowd.

The prototype is Episode 1, a 25-30 minute playable segment in 6 scenes, built over [9] months. It tests whether players notice and use these systems, measured with a choice log and three playtest rounds. The game runs on two plugins Phantom Break wrote for this project, which the studio's next game, ProjectSilence, will reuse.

### Revenue model

Premium game, one purchase for all five episodes, sold on Steam at [$__ USD] (draft, per `14-revenue-model.md`). A free demo of the Episode 1 opening builds wishlists before release. Production funding comes from [a publisher deal and the CMF Commercial Projects Program]. The revenue model gives low, middle and high scenarios of [__], [__] and [__] units in the first [two] years.

### Target audience

Adults [18-34] on PC who buy story-driven choice games such as Dispatch, Life is Strange and The Walking Dead, and side-on pixel-art games such as Replaced. They play in sessions of 30-90 minutes and replay to see other branches. Timed choices with a silent option suit streamers, whose audiences argue over each answer.

### Market

The game sells into the narrative adventure market on Steam. [One line from `15-market-study.md`: size of the narrative adventure segment on Steam, and the sales or player counts for Dispatch, Life is Strange and Replaced.] Project Runner competes on its systems rather than its budget: observation becomes dialogue, silence is counted, and consequence shows up in the world. Its HD-2D side-on look sets it apart from the 3D third-person games in the genre.
