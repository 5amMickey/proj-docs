## Draft answer

<!-- answer start -->
Phantom Break commits to the steps below on Project Runner and will report on each one at final costs. We follow the Canadian Media Producers Association's 2024 recommendations, including its guides "Federal, Provincial and Municipal Sustainability Opportunities (2024)" and "Animation Production Sustainability: A Case Study".

Remote work and travel. The team works from home studios, so there are no daily commutes. Meetings, reviews and most playtests happen online. When local travel is needed we will use public transit or electric vehicles. No flights are planned for the prototype.

Carbon calculation. We will estimate the prototype's emissions at the start and at the end, covering electricity for workstations and build machines, cloud services, and travel. The final report will include both figures and what changed.

Cleaner power and efficient equipment. Workstations and build machines will use power-saving settings and shut down when idle. Long renders and bakes will run in scheduled batches instead of keeping machines on. Smart power strips will cut standby power. We will extend the life of existing computers instead of buying new ones. When we must buy, we will choose refurbished or energy-efficient models.

Waste diversion. Retired hardware will be donated or sent to certified e-waste recycling. Prototype marketing will be digital only, with no printed materials or merchandise.

Training. At kickoff every team member will get a short briefing on these steps and on how to report hours of machine use and travel for the carbon estimate.

The game's city runs on parts salvaged from the wasteland outside it, and its lead repairs what others throw away. Repair and reuse are part of the story as well as the studio's practice.
<!-- answer end -->
