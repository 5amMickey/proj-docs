Phantom Break commits to the steps below on Project Runner and will report on each one at final costs.

Community relations. Our team has worked in the Calgary game community for years through the Calgary Game Developers Association (CGDA) and as instructors at Bow Valley College. Mike Lohaus, our marketing lead, will coordinate community work. He will run [two] public playtests at local events and [one] online playtest, and record the feedback and our responses.

Consultation. The story deals with disability and prosthetics, unlicensed medical care, poverty, and entering a city without papers. Before the script is locked we will pay a consultant with limb difference to review the outline and script, a nurse or paramedic to review the clinic scenes, and a sensitivity reader with lived migration experience to review Episode 1. We will log each review and what we changed.

Production and creative process. We will recruit from diverse communities for open roles, starting with the art director. Every team member will take cultural competency training.

Accessibility, accommodation and support. All dialogue will have subtitles with speaker names and adjustable text size. Timed choices will have a setting to lengthen or turn off the timer. Controls will be remappable, and no puzzle will rely on colour alone. At least [two] players with disabilities will test these features.

Policies, stewardship, accountability and communication. The consultation log and an accessibility checklist will live in the project repository, and both go into the final report.

Targeting new audiences. We will invite players with disabilities, and players new to story-driven games, to our playtests through Bow Valley College, the CGDA and [disability gaming group].

Accessibility, privacy or connectivity. The prototype collects no personal data. Playtest surveys will be anonymous and will follow Canada's privacy law (PIPEDA).
